# Two installed skills each define "thread" and "the owner" in their own words

## Summary

The planning skill and the design skill each give a definition of "thread" and of "the owner",
worded differently. The primer's `primer@coined-terms` points to one of them for each term, and
changes neither.

## Details

### What

- "thread": `skill@knowledge-architect-planning@planning-terms` says "one proposal of the
  discussion, carrying a state"; `skill@knowledge-architect-design@discussion-overview` says "one
  open question or proposal, the unit that carries state". The primer points to the design skill's.
- "the owner": `skill@knowledge-architect-planning@planning-terms` says "the person whose word
  closes a fork, and every question `primer@question-authority` reserves to the owner"; the design
  skill's overview says "the person whose word closes the questions" that its section on decision
  authority gives the owner. The primer points to the planning skill's.

Found by the reviews of the work that added the primer's terms section; not established whether a
session has acted differently on either pair.

### Why it matters

Two definitions of one term, worded apart, are two statements that can drift into two meanings,
against `goal@agent-skills@no-contradiction-in-workflow`; `design@agent-skills@term-defined-at-first-load`
gives each coined term one home, and these two have two.

### What would close it

Each term defined in one skill, the other skill pointing to it or restating it in one sentence with
its pointer, and the primer's entry pointing to the home kept.

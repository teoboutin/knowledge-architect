# No review compares the readings that several design heads take of one goal

## Summary

A design head states, beside its reference to a goal, the constraint it derives from that goal, per
`design@agent-skills@standing-argument-in-head`. No review reads that sentence against the
constraints the other heads citing the same goal state. A goal states a direction and leaves a
question of when, how much or by what means to design, per
`design@agent-skills@goal-one-meaning-any-breadth`, so two heads may read one goal in opposite
ways, and nothing reports it.

## Details

### What

The design discussion that brought the goal standard to three kinds decided a comparison: the
decision-record axis of a review compares the reading of each head a diff writes with the readings
of the other heads that cite the same goal, which `cargo klarch show` lists, and reports only two
readings that cannot both hold, since several heads may read one goal in ways that differ and
agree. It answered a cause of that discussion's premortem: two heads read one goal in opposite
directions.

It is not built. It is an instruction about how an agent behaves that rests on a prediction, which
`design@agent-skills@behaviour-additions-need-real-use` admits only on evidence from real use. The
owner ruled that it stands if the session has an argument that it leads to a better result in a
target project, and that an issue is fine if the session has doubts. The session has two:

- no instance of the failure is recorded. The two recorded cases are of one head's reading held
  apart from the goal and not found from it, in
  `issue@model@a-reference-to-an-anchor-s-own-directory-breaks-when-the-anchor-moves` and in
  `design@transcript@transcript-labels-in-code`; none is of two heads reading one goal in opposite
  ways, which is what a comparison among heads finds;
- its cost is known and its benefit is not. One goal of this repository was cited 38 times when
  the comparison was weighed, and `cargo klarch show` gives a file and a line for each citation,
  which a reviewer maps to its head by hand, per
  `issue@query@no-command-lists-the-standing-entries-citing-a-set-of-entries`.

The suspected mechanism: thinner goals leave more to each head's reading, and a head is written
against the goal's text, not against the readings other heads already took.

The rival the discussion refused, a register of goal interpretations, records an interpretation
apart from a decision, where it is argued against no rival.

### Why it matters

Two heads that read one goal in opposite directions both bind as presumptions, and each cites a
goal that binds outright, so a session grounding on one of them has no sign of the other. It
threatens `goal@knowledge-architect@agents-work-without-drift`.

### What would close it

A design discussion, opened on the owner's word, or when a review, an audit or a retrospective
finds two heads whose stated constraints from one goal cannot both hold: it decides whether a review compares the
readings, on which axis, and with which command of the checker. An instance found is its evidence;
until one is, the comparison stays unbuilt.

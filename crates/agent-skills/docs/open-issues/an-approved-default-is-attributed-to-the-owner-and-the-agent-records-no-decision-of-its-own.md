# A default the owner approved is attributed to the owner, and an agent has no way to record a decision as its own

## Summary

The owner does not want a default they approved, without analysing it deeply, attributed to them
long term, and wants agents more autonomous about design, defaults and records, with more of the
owner's intent moved into the goals. Three cases of one session meet the question.

## Details

### What

The owner's rulings at the retrospective intake of 2026-10-24, on three findings of one rootpoint
session that worked six slices of a milestone in one branch:

- An approval of a default put as a fork. `primer@design-heads` makes the owner's words a ground
  where "the owner chose where the argued rivals did not settle it", and the decision-recording
  skill makes the owner's choice the ground of a tie. The owner disagreed: an approval of a default
  is given without analysing the proposal deeply, and the owner does not want such a decision
  attributed to them long term. The owner suggested, as a hedged possibility, a path that lets an
  agent attribute the decision to itself, explicitly in the design head.
- A built default that narrows a head while it awaits the owner. The owner wants to change the
  condition of `skill@knowledge-architect-decision-recording@when-recording-happens`, "A default
  awaiting the owner is built as written … and enters a design head once the owner rules on it",
  so that agents are more autonomous about design, defaults and records, and more of the owner's
  intent moves to goals. The analysis read the case against the planning skill's sentence that "A
  scope change listed there does not start before its ruling", which the session had not applied,
  and against `issue@agent-skills@the-primer-s-divergence-paragraph-reads-two-ways-on-work-that-goes-on`,
  whose question the case answers in real use.
- A milestone whose last slice landed with six defaults unruled, which the planning skill had leave
  with its last slice; the rewording of that sentence was carried out, and the owner asked that the
  case be noted here.

The texts the question touches: `design@agent-skills@head-ground-is-the-argument`,
`design@agent-skills@unargued-approval-is-argued`, `design@agent-skills@owner-rules-at-a-fork`,
`design@agent-skills@default-ruled-before-merge` and `rejected@agent-skills@delegated-decision-state`.

The suspected mechanism, in one sentence: every decision a session cannot settle on the record goes
to the owner, and the owner's quick approval then becomes the head's ground.

Re-entry point: the design discussion this entry asks for, on the owner's word.

### Why it matters

A head grounded on an approval the owner gave quickly carries the owner's authority it does not
have, against `goal@knowledge-architect@the-owner-decides`; and every default held for the owner's
word slows the work, against `goal@knowledge-architect@agents-work-without-drift`.

### What would close it

A discussion under `skill@knowledge-architect-design` on who a decision is attributed to when the
owner approved a default, and when an agent records a decision as its own, with the goals the
owner's intent would move to written under `skill@knowledge-architect-goal-setting`.

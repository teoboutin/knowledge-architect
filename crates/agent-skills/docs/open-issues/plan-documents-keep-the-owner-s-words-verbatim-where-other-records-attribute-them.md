# Plan documents keep the owner's words verbatim, where every other durable record attributes them, and sessions slip between the two

## Summary

`primer@design-heads` has a durable record attribute a decision to the owner and never quote the
owner, and keeps the verbatim words for a plan document's items and a committed file that leaves.
Sessions keep slipping on the attribution side: a hedge dropped, a remark recorded as a ruling, a
quotation in a commit message. On the owner's suggestion, the two regimes may be what confuses
them.

## Details

### What

- `primer@design-heads`: "A head, a commit message, a tripwire, a `rejected` entry and an issue
  entry state what the owner decided and how far the decision reaches … never the owner's words
  verbatim. The verbatim words stay where the deliberation is kept: a plan document's items, and a
  committed file that leaves once its outcomes are carried out."
- `skill@knowledge-architect-planning`, the rows of a plan document's items: a thread carries "the
  owner's words that closed it, verbatim"; `design@agent-skills@plan-records-the-exchange` keeps
  "the owner's rulings verbatim".
- The instances, in the workflow files received at the retrospective intake of 2026-10-24: a hedge
  of the owner's dropped from a tripwire's premise and from two commit messages; a conditional
  hedge of the owner's narrowed to a plain condition; a hedged remark recorded as the owner's
  judgement; the owner's words quoted in a commit message, in three sessions of two projects; a
  batch-approved default attributed to the owner in a head. Six files of three projects, each
  caught before the merge, most by the transcript reviewer. A seventh session had a harness
  classifier refuse an edit that wrote the owner's closing words into a milestone document.

The suspected mechanism, the owner's hypothesis, hedged as the owner gave it: the planning skill's
demand for exact quotes is probably what confuses agents, who carry the habit of quoting into the
records that must attribute.

Re-entry point: a design discussion under `skill@knowledge-architect-design`, on the owner's word or
when work next changes how a plan document keeps the owner's words.

### Why it matters

An attribution that loses a hedge records a firmer ruling than the owner gave, against
`goal@knowledge-architect@the-owner-decides`; the transcript reviewer catches most of them, at a
review round each.

### What would close it

A discussion that keeps the two regimes, with the reason, or changes one: plan documents attribute
too, with the verbatim words left to the transcripts; or a single rule for both, stated where a
session writes either. Then the attribution slips of a release's retrospectives are counted
against these.

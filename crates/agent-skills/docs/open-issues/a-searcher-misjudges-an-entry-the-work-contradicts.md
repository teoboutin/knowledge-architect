# A record searcher grades `unrelated` or `constrains` an entry the work contradicts

## Summary

Two retrospectives report entries that a searcher of the record search graded below what they bore
on the work: one `unrelated` against a draft whose rule it contradicts, three `constrains, respect`
that another reading found `conflicts`. Each was found later, by reviewers or by an experiment's
cross-checkers. A miss by a searcher's judgement has no entry that collects it, while a miss on a
part added after the search does, so the evidence on the search's recall is read in no one place.

## Details

### What

The instances, from this repository's retrospective workflow files:

- the branch that closed the mutation survivors of three suites, 2026-11-16, its standing answer on
  missed entries: the head that a comment of two older tests cites, on why they compare a text
  whole, was graded `unrelated` against the in-change draft, whose rule it contradicts. The session
  put a question to the owner on a premise that head and comment defeat. The decision-record,
  design-conformance, self-consistency, code-claims and adversarial reviewers found it before the
  merge. The file does not name the head; the message of the commit "[review] A cause's text is
  compared whole for a claim of absence, and four issue entries the review found stale or missing
  are repaired", on main, names `design@agent-skills@plain-text-is-no-repair` as the reason of those
  tests, and the test `an_unanchored_path_shape_s_repair_offers_no_unchecked_form` in
  `path@check@src/check/references.rs` cites it beside a whole comparison today; that this is the
  head the searcher graded is an assumption, since an `unrelated` verdict writes no generated line;
- the branch that built the record search's saved workflow, 2026-11-16, its standing answer on
  missed entries: `design@agent-skills@work-divided-by-judgement`,
  `design@agent-skills@design-audit-checks-fit` and `goal@serves-one-kind-of-project`, graded
  `constrains, respect`, were refuted to `conflicts` by the cross-checkers of a stage tried as an
  experiment and rejected. The session missed them because its compact light lines left out the
  searchers' reasons; that cause is repaired, a light line keeping its reason per
  `design@query@light-line-keeps-its-reason`. Not established here: whether a reading of the work
  confirms each of the three refutations, which is what such a refutation counts for, per
  `design@agent-skills@synthetic-evidence-not-built`.

The suspected mechanism, in one sentence, an assumption: a searcher grades an entry against the
words of the draft, so an entry whose subject the draft does not name reads as `unrelated` or
`respect` even where the draft's rule contradicts it.

The retrospective's question on a missed entry tells a searcher's judgement from an entry or a
part of the work that came after the search, per `design@agent-skills@premortem-as-watch-points`,
and only the last cause has an entry, `issue@agent-skills@a-record-search-misses-what-is-added-after-it`.

A refuting stage run by the workflow on every search is a rejected design,
`rejected@agent-skills@verdicts-verified-in-the-workflow`; a shape that brings it back needs a new
argument.

Re-entry point: a design discussion that changes how a searcher grades, or the bound of
`design@query@worklist-bound-is-40000-words`; or a third retrospective that reports such a miss.

### Why it matters

`goal@records-reach-their-reader` asks that a recorded item reach the work it bears on, and
`design@query@worklist-bound-is-40000-words` states that no measurement of recall stands behind the
bound, which real sessions measure. A miss by judgement that reaches no entry is read again only in
the archived retrospective files, so the measurement the bound's head asks for is never assembled.

### What would close it

A discussion under `skill@knowledge-architect-design` weighs the instances against the searcher's
grading instructions, in `agent@knowledge-architect-record-searcher`, and against the bound, and
changes one of them, or rules that no change is worth its cost, the instances stated with the
ruling.

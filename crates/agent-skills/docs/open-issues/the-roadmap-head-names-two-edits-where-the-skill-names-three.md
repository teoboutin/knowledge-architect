# The head on the roadmap's order names two edits that need no word, where the planning skill names three

## Summary

`design@agent-skills@roadmap-order-is-the-owners` says that two edits of a roadmap row need no word:
rewriting the row of an issue a new plan document closes, and removing the row of a plan document
that leaves. The planning skill, `skill@knowledge-architect-planning@spec-or-milestone`, names three:
the third is removing the row of an issue closed with no plan document scheduling it.

## Details

### What

The skill's third edit came with the commit "[audit] Pass 2 of the agentic-workflow axis: 70 findings
confirmed, 37 applied, 30 ruled by the owner, 3 issues", whose message holds it as a finding applied
to the skill: "a roadmap row of an issue closed with no plan leaves with no word". The head's
sentence stayed, so the two texts differ. The skill gives its reason, that the work is done, and
`primer@where-knowledge-goes` states the same exit in its row on the roadmap. The head's reason
for its two edits is that they "keep a row pointing at the same work", which the third, the end of
that work, does not do.

`design@agent-skills@roadmap-orders-issues`, which the head cites, also names the two edits alone.

### Why it matters

A session that reads the head can hold that the third edit needs the owner's word, while the skill
has it removed by the commit that closes the issue. The head's statement is wrong as the workflow is
built, or the skill's third edit has no argument in the record.

### What would close it

The head's statement, and that of `design@agent-skills@roadmap-orders-issues` where it names the
edits, brought to three through `skill@knowledge-architect-design`; or the third edit argued against
there, and the skill and the primer brought back to two.

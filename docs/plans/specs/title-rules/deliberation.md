## Arguments

### The owner doubts there is a problem, and holds that a title must not restate its head and may be imprecise as long as it does not mislead `##arg-1`

Round 1, the owner. Bears on `thread@title-rules@title-tripwire-verdict`,
`thread@title-rules@title-misleads-bar`, `thread@title-rules@title-length-bound`. The owner said
that the owner was not yet convinced there is any problem; that the strictness of the title rules must not

much of a problem as long as it does not mislead.

### A definition of "mislead" a reviewer can apply, and what stays a finding `##arg-6`

### "None reached main" suffers from survivor bias, and tripwires that count review findings invert the signal `##arg-9`

### The rule on slips lets a clause fire on findings the reviews report, against condition 3 `##arg-12`

The reply to round 2, the session. Bears on `thread@title-rules@review-finding-is-no-firing`.
"Condition 3 says "A clause that counts what the decision instructs or endorses fires on the
decision working". A review that reports a lapse is the workflow doing what it instructs. Yet the
rule on slips still lets a clause fire "on the same failure recurring in separate pieces of work",
even when every instance was reported. That is the inversion you describe: the more reliably
reviews catch a lapse, the sooner the tripwire fires." / "The signal that something is wrong is the
opposite: a lapse that reached main with no procedure reporting it, found later by an audit, by
another branch's review, or in use."

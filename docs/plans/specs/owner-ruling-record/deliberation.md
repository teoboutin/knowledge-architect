## Arguments

### Deferring the head causes two problems and keeps nothing `##arg-1`

Round 1, the owner. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@head-written-with-the-code` and
`thread@owner-ruling-record@review-timing`. The owner saw no reason to defer the head until the
ruling. The deferral causes two problems: a history where heads land long after their code; and
trouble at review time, where the head is either not written and so not judged, or written and
judged as unruled, then removed and added again once the owner rules. The owner judged that the
deferral brings no value, and that the review should perhaps always wait for the owner's word.

### The ruling does not change the head's text `##arg-2`

Round 1, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@head-written-with-the-code` and
`thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. "Your ruling does not change the head's
ground. The table in `primer@design-heads` says your approval of a session default is \"no
ground\". […] So a head written at build time would already have its final text. Only the clause
\"the owner approved it\" is missing: If you approve, that clause is added. If you choose the other
shape, the code changes anyway, and the head is rewritten in the same change. Deferring the head
therefore keeps nothing out of the record."

### The primer holds back only the merge, not the review `##arg-3`

Round 1, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@review-timing`. "the primer holds back
only the merge, not the review. If the head is written at build time, the review judges it, and
your ruling only adds the clause."

### The deferral is a leftover, and only facts and costs are worth marking as the owner's `##arg-4`

Round 2, the owner. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@head-written-with-the-code` and
`thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. The owner said the wait for the head comes
from the time when rulings were always recorded in heads. Today only the approval is recorded, and
the owner saw little value even in that: only facts and costs are worth marking as the owner's
words. The owner asked the discussion to keep in mind that some instructions in the tree are
leftovers of parts of the workflow that were since reworked.

### Only a real fork should take a built default `##arg-5`

Round 2, the owner. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@what-takes-a-built-default`. The owner took
back the remark of round 1 on reducing what waits on the owner's word. Of the cases the session
listed, the owner judged that only a real fork can take a built default: building on a fact or a
cost the owner has not ranked can cause problems. The owner allowed one exception: where the
recorded design or the goals already give leads on how such a cost would rank, the session may
proceed on its judgement.

### The history shows the deferral's reason is gone `##arg-6`

Round 2, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@head-written-with-the-code`. "The deferral
sentence came in on 2026-10-12, in \"The findings the retrospective analyses ruled to handle now
are handled\". The primer of that tree said \"The owner's words are a ground, quoted, only where the
decision came from the owner\". […] A head whose text depended on your words could not be written
before your words existed. That was the sentence's reason." / "That reason is gone. Today
`design@agent-skills@heads-stand-on-argument` and `design@agent-skills@fork-choice-in-weak-form`
record a fork's choice as an attribution in the weak form, and an approval as \"no ground\"."

### The weak form reopens the same way whoever chose `##arg-7`

Round 2, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. "the weak form
reopens in the same way whoever chose, so the attribution changes no later move.
`rejected@agent-skills@fork-choice-reversed-only-by-the-owner` already argues that an approval does
not show a weighing you made." Cost: "the transcript reviewer can no longer check that an approval
was recorded. It still checks that no pick of yours is recorded wrong." The plan review of code
claims found the citation misplaced: the argument is in the last paragraph of
`design@agent-skills@fork-choice-in-weak-form`, not in the `rejected` entry.

### Every fork is already the weighing of an unranked cost `##arg-8`

Round 2, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@what-takes-a-built-default`. "The primer
defines a fork as two shapes that meet every constraint, \"so that choosing between them weighs a
cost or a scope that no goal or decision ranks\". Under that wording, every fork is the weighing of
an unranked cost. Your sentence must therefore draw a line inside today's forks." Reading A, by the
size of the cost: "the session has to judge whether you might refuse a cost, and that is the same
judgement you are taking back from it." Reading B, by the shape of the question: "most
single-shape questions with a cost already go to a goal, a scope, or to \"a cost alone makes no
fork\"."

### A lead is weaker than what lets the session build today `##arg-9`

Round 2, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@what-takes-a-built-default`. "Today
`design@agent-skills@derived-decision-proceeds-on-default` lets the session build without asking
only where the record **settles** the question, which means it is no fork. A lead is weaker. […] If
a lead is enough to proceed, the session decides at forks on analogy. It would still list such a
decision under \"Decided\", where you can contest it."

### Waiting for every ruling before the review is circular `##arg-10`

Round 2, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@review-timing`. "Waiting for every ruling
before the review runs is circular: the review produces new defaults of its own, its `D<n>`
findings. With #head-written-with-the-code, a review run before your rulings already judges every
head in its final text when you approve. When you pick the other shape, the repair is reviewed as a
repair." The rival, a review after every ruling: "Cost: a second full review whenever the first one
produces findings."

### "Final text" read strictly makes an unending chain of reviews `##arg-11`

Round 3, the owner. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@review-timing` and
`thread@owner-ruling-record@head-written-with-the-code`. The owner judged that the criterion "the
review before the merge judges every head the branch lands, in its final text" cannot be followed
strictly: a review can lead to repairs, and a strict reading would call for a review of each
repair, without end. The owner asked for a weaker form of "final text".

### The built-intent head excludes recording at spec time, not during the work `##arg-17`

### Shape A along the work against shape B at the end, and the session's default A `##arg-18`

Round 3, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@harvest-timing`. Shape A, for: "every
commit holds the code and the design it states; the review of the work (point 4) judges code and
heads together, so the separate review of the harvest disappears; the head is written while the
implementing session still holds the step's details." Cost: "a decision whose code spans several
steps gets its head at the step that completes it, or gets amended at each step; a later step or a
review that changes the decision rewrites a head already on the branch". Shape B, for: "each head is
written once, from the plan document, after every step has settled the shape." Cost: "the review of
the work never sees the heads; the decision exists only in the plan document until the last
commits." "I find no fact that defeats either shape. It is a weighing […]. That makes it a fork,
yours to rule. My default is **A**. Its first benefit holds for every step, while the rewrite cost
arises only where a later step changes a decision."

### No choice is attributed; only an underivable premise is, by its initial provenance `##arg-19`

Round 4, the owner. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. The owner judged
that if the owner's approval is not recorded, no attribution to the session should be either: a
generic "X chosen over Y because …" is better, since any attribution of a choice is useless there.
An attribution becomes useful only for what cannot be derived without knowledge from outside the
project, such as a fact, the ranking of a cost, or a judgement of what is plausible or of expected
use. Such a premise can be attributed to the owner or to the session, by where it first came from,
not by who approved it.

### The owner's weighing of the harvest's two shapes `##arg-20`

Round 4, the owner. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@harvest-timing`. The owner wants to avoid
decisions amended at each step of the work. Doing all the record work together at the end may give
better-written heads, where shape A gives the implementer one more duty, writing heads to the
standard, beside the code. The owner granted that the review of the work gains from seeing the
design record, but judged that the spec should already hold most of what that review needs.

### Provenance routes a defeated premise, and a choice needs none `##arg-21`

Round 4, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. "**Why
provenance is needed there and nowhere else.** The routing of a defeated premise depends on it: A
fact or ranking **you** reported gives way to its source, and you are told at the top of the turn,
per `primer@owner-word-premise`. If it was a ranking of intent, the case is a contradiction put to
you. A premise the **session assumed** is corrected on the evidence, and the correction goes under
\"Decided\". Without the attribution, a later session cannot tell which of the two routes applies. A
choice needs no attribution, because the weak form reopens it in the same way whoever made it. The
same holds for an argument: it is judged on its merits, so \"the rule is the owner's proposal\" or
\"the owner's argument\" carries nothing either."

### Converting the old lines is audit work, not a sweep `##arg-22`

Round 4, the session. Bears on `thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. "Part of these
lines are premises that keep their attribution. Each line needs a judgement of whether its premise
can be derived, so the cleanup is audit work, not a sweep. My revised default: convert a head when a
change touches it, as `design@agent-skills@existing-heads-on-touch` already says; the design-record
audit's standard gains the rule, so its next pass converts the rest, run when you choose. The rival
is a sweep in this branch, by a dispatched agent: one branch with a single form, at the cost of a
judgement on 150 to 180 lines landing in a review that is not an audit."

### A new member is routed by who chose, so a fork's ranking keeps its provenance `##arg-24`

Round 5, the session, on a material finding of the record search. Bears on
`thread@owner-ruling-record@approval-left-unrecorded`. "**This finding is material:
`design@agent-skills@extension-follows-the-ground` routes a new member by who chose.** […] It reads
who chose from the head itself. My draft wrote a fork's choice as \"X over Y: <cost> weighed against
<cost>\", with no provenance. A later session could then find who chose only in commits and plan
documents, and that is exactly `rejected@agent-skills@member-routed-by-approval-scope`, which was
rejected for that cost. The correction comes from your own rule. A fork's choice is a ranking of
costs that no goal ranks, which is one of the premises you said should keep its provenance. So the
weighing is written with its provenance: \"X over Y: the owner ranked <cost> above <cost>\" or \"the
session ranked …\". An approval adds nothing: an approved session ranking stays the session's."

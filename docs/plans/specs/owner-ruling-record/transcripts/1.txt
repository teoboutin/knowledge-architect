## The texts

- **Primer, `primer@question-authority`:** "A default put to the owner is built, and the branch that builds it merges once the owner has ruled on it".
- **Decision-recording, `skill@knowledge-architect-decision-recording`, lines 32–35:** "A default awaiting the owner is built as written, … and enters a design head once the owner rules on it. Until then it is no approved decision, and a head is read against the owner's approval before it is written".
- **Planning, `skill@knowledge-architect-planning`, lines 843–844:** a plan document cannot leave until "every default the document lists as awaiting the owner is ruled, since a design head records such a default only once the owner has ruled on it".
- **Review, `skill@knowledge-architect-review`, lines 179–181:** when planning writes an item as a default awaiting you, that listing is the item's outcome, and the record of the review names it "as awaiting the owner's word".

## Which of your questions take a built default

The primer reserves five kinds of question to you. Row 1 of `primer@met-outside-the-task` and `skill@knowledge-architect-design` (lines 80–96) split them:

| question reserved to you | default built, work goes on? |
| --- | --- |
| **a fork**: two or more shapes meet every constraint, and choosing weighs a cost or scope that no goal ranks | **yes** |
| **a fact only you hold** (a plan, something you observed) | **yes**, built on an assumed fact |
| **a goal to set or change** | **yes**, but the goal stands as it is meanwhile, so no head waits on it |
| a contradiction no argument resolves (including a ruling whose premise proved false) | no: the part that rests on it waits |
| an action that cannot be undone | no: only the reversible part is built |

Two more cases appear in the lists of defaults but do not follow the build-then-rule pattern:

- **A scope change**: an answer that relaxes one of your rulings or turns it from its direction (`skill@knowledge-architect-planning`, `##working-a-slice`). It is listed as a default awaiting you, but it "does not start before its ruling".
- **A new member of a head whose members you named at a fork**: "nothing of it is written before the owner's word" (`primer@design-heads`).

In practice, the head that waits on you is almost always a **fork**, sometimes a **fact only you hold**. Forks reach you through these routes:

1. a design discussion's forks, written as the `D<n>` defaults of a plan document;
2. a design audit's "choice between shapes" gap (planning, lines 657–661);
3. a review finding that is a fork, written as a `D<n>`;
4. bounded work's single proposal (design skill, line 202), which goes on unless you stop it;
5. an outcome you hold in an audit, a retrospective intake or a review.

## Waiting adds nothing to the head

Your ruling does not change the head's ground. The table in `primer@design-heads` says your approval of a session default is "no ground". It is recorded as "the session chose X over Y, which no goal ranks; the owner approved it".

So a head written at build time would already have its final text. Only the clause "the owner approved it" is missing:

- If you approve, that clause is added.
- If you choose the other shape, the code changes anyway, and the head is rewritten in the same change.

Deferring the head therefore keeps nothing out of the record. What it does is:

- separate the head from its code in the history;
- hide the decision from the review, or have the review flag the head as unruled.

These are the two problems you named.

## The two levers

1. **Write the head when the code lands, marked as unruled.** This drops the deferral in decision-recording and planning, and changes what the decision-record reviewer checks.
2. **Shrink the set.** Most of these questions are forks. You could narrow what counts as a fork you hold, for example to forks whose choice reaches a consumer (an interface, a format, a command line). The session would then decide the rest in the weak form, and they would appear only in the turn's "Decided" section.

Your remark that "the review should always wait for my word" fits the primer already: the primer holds back only the merge, not the review. If the head is written at build time, the review judges it, and your ruling only adds the clause.

## #approval-left-unrecorded, reshaped by your proposal

You proposed this shape; here it is with the slots filled in. A durable record (a head, a `rejected` entry, a tripwire, an issue) attributes **no choice** to anyone. It states "X over Y, because …", or, at a fork, "X over Y: the weighing of <cost> against <cost>". **Only a premise that cannot be derived from inside the project is attributed**, by where it first came from:

- a fact;
- the ranking of a cost;
- a judgement of what is plausible, or of expected use.

Each such premise is written "the owner reported" or "the session assumed". If you approve a premise the session supplied, it is still the session's.

**Why provenance is needed there and nowhere else.** The routing of a defeated premise depends on it:

- A fact or ranking **you** reported gives way to its source, and you are told at the top of the turn, per `primer@owner-word-premise`. If it was a ranking of intent, the case is a contradiction put to you.
- A premise the **session assumed** is corrected on the evidence, and the correction goes under "Decided".

Without the attribution, a later session cannot tell which of the two routes applies. A choice needs no attribution, because the weak form reopens it in the same way whoever made it. The same holds for an argument: it is judged on its merits, so "the rule is the owner's proposal" or "the owner's argument" carries nothing either.

**The deliberation records are not touched.** The plan document's Threads ("the owner's ruling that closed it, attributed"), its `D<n>` list marked as ruled, and the commit message keep who proposed and who ruled. The transcript reviewer matches those records against the transcript. Its check on the durable records becomes: no premise attributed to the wrong source, and no choice attributed at all.

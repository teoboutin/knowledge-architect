# The goal of a tool for a task states no limit to tasks that repeat, which the owner holds

## Summary

`goal@tasks-a-tool-does-better-are-the-tool-s` reaches any task a tool does at a lower cost or with
a better result, once the owner takes it in. The owner holds that it reaches only tasks that repeat,
so a one-shot task, such as a consumer's migration at a pin move, is out of its scope. The goal's
words do not say so.

## Details

### What

At the design audit of the slice that made the register `goal` the root's alone, the owner ruled
that a consumer's respelling of its goal references gets no tool, on the ground that the goal is for
repeated tasks and a one-shot migration is out of its scope. At the review before that slice's
merge, the owner stated that this is a general limit of the goal, and chose not to draft it into the
goal that day.

The goal's text names no repetition. It reaches "a task whose result follows with no judgement from
what the project holds", and searching, updating and verifying what a project holds, and it leaves
each task to the owner's decision to take it in. The word comes from a former goal of xtask, which
asked for a tool for what this repository's own instructions require repeatedly.

### Why it matters

A session that reads the goal, and not this entry, takes any task that a tool does better, a
one-shot one included, as within the goal, and may propose a tool on that ground, which the owner
then refuses. A decision argued from the goal, such as `design@query@id-lookup-command` or
`design@mutate@mutation-command`, cites a scope wider than the owner's.

### What would close it

A goal-setting session, under `skill@knowledge-architect-goal-setting`, drafts the limit to tasks
that repeat into `goal@tasks-a-tool-does-better-are-the-tool-s`, and the owner rules on the wording.
Re-entry point: the next proposal of a tool for a one-shot task, the next review of the whole goal
set, or earlier on the owner's word.
